    About

    An independent practice tool

    StudySprint is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by College Board. AP is a trademark owned by College Board.

    Where content comes from

    Study guides and practice questions are original, written for StudySprint. They do not copy or closely paraphrase College Board exam questions, answer keys, scoring guidelines, AP Classroom materials, or the Student Question Bank, and no College Board test content is sent to any AI service. Official free-response questions are linked on College Board's own pages and never copied here. Every guide, question, and video records its source, rights status, and verification date.

    What is verified

    Course names and page addresses come from AP Central's course list. Unit titles come from College Board's course pages and frameworks for the courses that have an outline here. The catalog changes over time, so StudySprint does not assume a fixed number of courses. The file CONTENT_STATUS.md in the project lists exactly what is complete.

    What a practice test is

    A StudySprint practice test is a short unit test: a fixed set of at least 8 of that unit's original questions that includes every topic in the unit and all three difficulty levels. It is not a full AP exam, and its score cannot predict an AP score. Two tests in the same unit never share questions, so a second test is genuinely different; reshuffling the same questions is never presented as a new test. Many units have only one test so far, because their question banks are still small; each unit page shows exactly what is available. Timed mode uses StudySprint's own pacing, not an official AP time limit. The test's look and flow are StudySprint's own; it is not a copy of any College Board testing app.

    How progress works

    Progress counts submitted answers only. Your first answer to a question is its first attempt. Later answers are retries and are tracked separately. Accuracy figures are estimates based on the questions you attempted. They cannot show that you have learned a topic or predict an exam score. The review plan's step checklist is your own schedule, and it is never counted as evidence of learning. The confidence rating in “Learn first” is self-reported too: it only decides which resources are suggested. Warm-up answers count like any other practice answers.

    Outside learning resources

    “Learn first” links to free resources that were checked before being listed: Khan Academy and OpenStax for AP Calculus AB, and existing video links for AP Physics 1. Each link is labelled unit-specific or general. Nothing from them is copied or embedded.

    Where your results are kept

    Results are saved in this browser, on this device, using local storage. Clearing site data or switching devices resets them. StudySprint asks for no name or account and sends nothing anywhere.

    An unfinished practice set or study session is saved as you go, so refreshing the page does not lose your place. On the My data page you can download a backup file of your progress, restore it in another browser or on another device, and undo a restore. A backup holds answer results, unit settings, and completed study sessions; unfinished sets stay on the device where they were started.

    Why practice by answering

    Trying to recall and apply ideas helps learning more than rereading. One study with college students found retrieval practice produced greater learning than concept mapping for the science texts it used. Read the study.
