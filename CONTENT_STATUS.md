# StudySprint content status

Generated by `python3 tools/build.py` on 2026-10-11 from the files in `data/`. Do not edit by hand: change the data and run the build again. The build fails if a unit's declared status does not match what its files contain.

StudySprint is a review site, not a complete course or textbook. Guides are short review notes, and practice questions are original.

## What the statuses mean

- **practice-ready**: an original review guide (at least 3 concept sections, a worked example, common mistakes) and at least 8 original multiple-choice questions that include introductory, standard, and challenging levels. Each question has an explanation for the correct answer and for every wrong choice, plus source and rights fields.
- **in progress**: some content exists but not enough to meet that bar.
- **outline only**: official unit titles (and official topic names where they were verified) are listed. No guide or questions have been written, so the site does not offer them.

## Study planner

A planner session has **5 steps**, defined in `data/planner.json`. The planner interface, the saved session history, and this report all read that file. The planner opens from the selected course and unit and draws its guide section and questions from that unit.

| # | Step | Share of the time | Uses | Produces question evidence? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review a guide section | 20% | One section of the unit's review guide | no |
| 2 | Answer practice questions | 35% | A randomized set of the unit's questions | yes |
| 3 | Review explanations for missed questions | 15% | Explanations for questions missed in the practice step | no |
| 4 | Try missed concepts with different questions | 20% | Different questions on the concepts missed earlier | yes |
| 5 | Results and next step | 10% | A summary of the session's submitted answers | no |

- **Plan completion** is the student's own checklist: the results step shows "x of 5 steps marked done". It is self-reported and never counted as evidence of learning.
- **Evidence** comes only from submitted answers. A session meets the practice threshold with at least 5 different questions answered and 70% or more correct on first attempts. A student can tick all 5 steps and still not meet it, and the results say so.
- Sessions saved before this change counted only the first 4 steps; the session history still shows those as "of 4".

## Learn first (optional beginner path)

Each practice-ready unit has an optional Learn first page: a 1 to 5 confidence rating (self-reported, never counted as evidence), a 3-question warm-up drawn from the unit's introductory questions, and a panel of learning resources. Resources are recommended when the rating is 1 or 2 and no more than 1 of the 3 warm-up questions is correct. They are also offered after two wrong first attempts in a row anywhere in practice or in the planner. From there the student can return to the same unit for easier practice (introductory and standard questions only, starting at the introductory level, without changing their saved range). Warm-up answers count as ordinary practice answers.

Every outside link records whether it is **unit-specific** or **general**, and the build rejects a course page, textbook, or collection page labelled unit-specific.

## Saving, backup, and hosting

These describe how the site behaves. They do not change any course content.

- **Progress stays in the browser.** No accounts, no server, no paid or outside services. Progress saved by earlier versions of the site is still read.
- **Backup and restore (My data page).** A student can download progress as a JSON file or copy it as text. A backup includes answer results (first try and each retry), unit settings (adaptive level, difficulty range, questions seen in the current round, confidence rating, last warm-up result), and completed study-session history. It does not include unfinished practice sets or planner sessions, which stay on the device where they were started; answers already submitted in them are included. A restore checks every field, drops anything it does not recognise, rejects files over 2 MB or in an unknown format, and shows what the backup contains and what the device will have afterwards before anything changes. Merge and replace are both offered. The previous progress is kept so the last restore, unit reset, or "clear all" can be undone.
- **How merge treats overlaps.** A question on both sides keeps one whole record, not a combination: the one with more attempts, or the more recent one if the counts are equal. A unit on both sides keeps the device's adaptive level, difficulty range, and seen-question list; only a newer confidence rating or warm-up result comes from the backup. A study session in both is listed once, and the 50 most recent are kept. The restore preview states these rules and how many shared questions and units each rule affects.
- **Unfinished work survives a refresh.** An open practice set (including review sets) or a planner session is saved as the student goes. Refreshing the page returns to the same question; after an answered question it moves on to the next one. The home page lists unfinished sets with Resume and Discard. Step timers restart from the full step time, and the 3-question Learn first warm-up is not saved mid-way.
- **Load errors.** If the course list, a course's unit list, a unit's content, or the planner settings fail to load, the request is retried once automatically; if it still fails, the page says what could not be loaded and offers Try again. A unit that fails to load is never shown as if it had no content.
- **Hosting.** All asset and data paths are relative, so the site works from a GitHub Pages project subpath (for example `/studysprint/`). The build writes a version stamp onto the CSS, script, and data requests so a new release is not hidden by cached files, and adds a `.nojekyll` file.
- **Accessibility and phones.** Each view has its own page title and level-1 heading, Back and Forward move between views, answer errors are linked to the question and announced, focus moves to each new question and each result, the course search announces how many courses match, buttons and answer choices are at least 44 px tall, and tables stack into labelled rows on narrow screens so pages do not scroll sideways at 320 px.

## Known gaps

- No individual College Board free-response question is matched to a unit. Doing so would require reading College Board exam content, which this project does not do, and most free-response questions span several units. Each unit links to the course's official past-exam-questions page instead. Any future `frqs` entry must be labelled approximate or spans-units unless the match is exact.
- Units with no verified unit-specific outside resource (the interface says so and points to the review guide): AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 2, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 3, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 4, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 5, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 6, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 7, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 8.
- 5 video links point to a general collection page that lists the video, not to the video itself, because a direct address could not be verified: AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 1, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 2, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 3, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 4, AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Unit 7. They are labelled general.
- Question review: every question is original. Numeric answers, the values behind wrong choices that come from a specific mistake, and every worked example were checked by computation; conceptual questions were checked by reading only. No question has been reviewed by a subject teacher, so a teacher review is recommended before classroom use.
- Learn first links: Khan Academy pages are rendered by JavaScript, so they were verified by confirming the address loads with a matching canonical URL and by the page title in a search index, not by watching each video.
- Courses beyond the outlined ones (AP Computer Science A and the rest) have no verified unit outline yet.
- Not yet checked on real devices or with screen readers: layout, touch targets, and keyboard behaviour were checked in an automated desktop browser at phone widths (320, 375, 390 px). Still needed: VoiceOver on an iPhone (Safari), TalkBack on an Android phone (Chrome), NVDA or JAWS on Windows, and a check that downloading and choosing a backup file works on iOS and Android.
